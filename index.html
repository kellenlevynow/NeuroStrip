02The idea

Run the demand engine the way NeuroStrip reads a muscle.

The story Control Bionics tells teams is about a measure that looked fine and a signal that did not. Demand generation has the same trap. Lead volume is the force plate: easy to hit, easy to trust, and able to hide a pipeline that is compensating. This plan instruments the signal.

In the pipeline illustration

Not healthy. Cheap, wrong-fit leads were covering for the missing ones.

Measure the signal

Every lead carries its audience, message, channel and campaign into Salesforce and through to revenue. Cost per lead stays off the scorecard. As a diagnostic, it is never shown without the sales-accepted rate beside it.

Feed the constraint

The bottleneck is not traffic. It is one inside-sales calendar, a finite pool of pilot kits and a thin proof library. Marketing sends the best 20 conversations a week, not the most.

Earn the scale

Spend follows evidence. More than a third of the budget buys proof and measurement before it buys reach, and media grows only as tests clear rules written in advance.

Six mental models the plan leans on

  • Output is not signalJudge the system by what happens underneath the easy number.
  • Theory of constraintsA system moves at the speed of its tightest point. Find it, feed it, then widen it.
  • The 95:5 ruleAbout 5% of buyers are in-market at any time. Capture them with search. Build memory with the rest so they call first.
  • Awareness before askMatch the message to what the buyer already knows. No demo ads to people who do not know the problem exists.
  • Barbell budgetMost of the money in named accounts and proven capture. A small, capped share in bets that could change the slope.
  • Land and expandOne clinic, 30 days, agreed criteria. Then the group.

05Positioning and awareness

Force shows what the body produced. NeuroStrip shows which muscle produced it.

NeuroStrip does not have an awareness problem. It has a category problem. The people who should buy it already believe they measure readiness, so nobody is searching for what it does. The message has to open a gap before it can offer to close it.

What they believe

"We already test for this."

Patients plateau or stop coming. Athletes re-injure. Reimbursement is squeezed and it is hard to stand out. But strength and function get tested, so readiness is covered.

What they do not see yet

Output hides compensation.

Force plates, dynamometers and hop tests measure what the limb produced. A limb can pass while the injured muscle is still under-recruiting and its neighbors cover for it.

What changes their mind

Seeing it on a screen.

Two graphs, one patient, one minute. The demonstration is the argument. Everything upstream exists to earn that minute.

Positioning statement

For clinicians and performance staff who decide when someone is ready, NeuroStrip is the wearable activation sensor that shows which muscles are doing the work, live, in the clinic or on the field.

Unlike force plates and dynamometers, which measure output, or lab EMG, which needs a lab, it goes on like a bandage and puts activation next to the measures you already trust.

Four muscles at once No wires, no gel Live on a tablet A record you can document

Scenario

Stretch keeps media spend the same and assumes three things go right: outbound is warmed and widened, a second weekly demo fills, and event follow-up lands.

Benchmarks behind the defaults: LinkedIn cost per lead averages $276 and runs $400 to $600 in healthcare technology; search averages $5.42 a click and an 8.2% click-to-lead rate across industries; cold email averages a 3.4% reply rate, 5.5% in the top quartile. Funnel rates and contract value are placeholders until the trial backfill. Calls booked near Day 90 become customers around Day 150, after a 30-day pilot.

Lever 1

Outbound, warmed and widened

In the stretch case outbound doubles from 2.7 to 5.4 calls a week at no extra media cost: warmed accounts hold about a third more calls per 100 contacts, and the sequence widens from 300 to 450 contacts a week. On direct response alone LinkedIn runs at about twice the allowable cost per call, so this lift is how it earns its budget. The holdout test tells us by Day 60.

Lever 2

A second weekly live demo

One-to-many time scales without touching the inside-sales calendar. A second slot aimed at Tier A clinic directors is the cheapest capacity in the plan. In the stretch case registrants rise from 60 to 90 a month and 18% book, which moves demos from 2.1 to 3.7 calls a week.

Lever 3

The event follow-up

With a December start, APTA CSM lands in the scale phase. Pre-booked meetings, a booth test people queue for and a 48-hour follow-up rule turn one week into a month of calls. In the stretch case 200 qualified conversations a quarter and a 22% booking rate move events from 2.3 to 3.4 calls a week.

    14Experimentation engine

    Twelve tests in the queue. Rules written first.

    A test without a decision rule is a campaign with a nicer name. Each of these has a hypothesis, one variable, one metric, a cap on spend or time, and an agreed response to every outcome before it launches.

    Kill

    The cap is reached and the leading metric is under half of control, or the share of leads from Tier A and B is under 20%.

    Spend moves the same day.

    Continue

    Within 20% of control either way. Run one more cycle or change exactly one variable.

    Two cycles without separation and it is retired.

    Scale

    30% or more better than control on the phase metric, with no drop in sales-accepted rate.

    Into always-on, plus 25% a week.

    Honest statistics for a small market

    With a thousand accounts and a few dozen leads a month, nothing reaches significance on lead counts, and pretending otherwise produces confident nonsense. So the tests compare big differences, such as message, offer and persona, not button colors. Each phase is read on the deepest metric that has enough volume:

    • Phase 1Engaged Tier A accounts
    • Phase 2Booked calls and fit rate
    • Phase 3Sales-accepted calls and pilots

    The backlog

    Ranked by impact, confidence and ease.

    One test, written out

    Experiment 4: does LinkedIn earn its place?

    This one decides where a quarter of the budget goes, so it gets the cleanest design in the plan.

    Hypothesis
    Accounts warmed by LinkedIn reply to outbound at least 20% more often.
    Design
    Tier A accounts inside the current outbound footprint are split in half at random, by account. One half gets outbound only. The other gets the same sequences plus LinkedIn. Same weeks, same senders.
    Metric
    Positive reply rate by account. Second: held meetings per 100 accounts.
    Cap
    Six weeks, or 150 accounts in each half.
    Owner
    Demand gen designs and reads it. The outbound partner keeps the halves clean.

    If the lift is under 10%

    LinkedIn prospecting drops to retargeting only. The money moves to search, live demos and events.

    If it is 10% to 20%

    Keep spend flat. Test whether the lift is concentrated in one persona before deciding.

    If it is 20% or more

    LinkedIn rises 25% a week inside frequency caps, and the design repeats as outbound widens.
