Follow one group

Winning a 12-clinic group's first clinic takes about 13 weeks and, on average, about $6,900 of marketing.

Here is how an example group, a made-up physical therapy (PT) company that owns 12 clinics, would move through the plan, and what each step costs at the plan's planning numbers. The rest of the page explains the system behind it.

$6,900 of marketing, on average, to win one new customer from the calls the recommended $90,000 budget pays for

We plan on one clinic paying Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, about $625 a month, so the $6,900 is covered by about 11 months of that clinic's payments, before product, sales and pilot kit costs.1

Where the $6,900 comes from

At the base budget, month three spends about $33,300. The plan expects about 15 qualified calls a week at Day 90, but about 4 of those already come in today and about 2 come from the February conference, which has its own budget. The spend pays for about 9 a week, or about 39 a month, so each costs about $860. Of qualified calls, 55% become real sales opportunities, 45% of those start a pilot, and half of pilots sign. So about 12.4% of calls become customers, and $860 divided by 0.124 is about $6,900 per new customer.

Counting every call, including the ones this budget does not pay for, the same month costs about $510 a call and $4,100 a customer. We use the higher figures, because new money only buys the calls it pays for.

This group's own share is about $61 plus the box. The $6,900 also pays for the groups that never buy: about 8 qualified calls lead to each customer.

How the per-group costs are worked out

  • List and data: the $10,800 data line spread over about 1,000 target groups is about $11 a group. It also pays for scheduling tools and Salesforce work.
  • Ads: the $22,500 account-based line spread over the roughly 500 groups in the half that sees ads is about $45 a group, about $27 of ads plus this group's share of Propensity's $9,000 fee.
  • Email: the $5,400 email line spread over all 1,000 groups is about $5 a group.
  • The direct mail box has no published price. It is quoted in week one and capped inside the small-tests line.

Learn more about the test design

The three tests

How each test runs

  • Inside each cluster, contacts are split at random between message A and message B, through the Propensity ad audiences and the outbound email sequences.
  • Each test is read at about 150 contacts per side, in week 8. If neither message is 30% ahead, it runs to six weeks, about 300 a side, and closes by February 15, the end of week 11.
  • We read two numbers: positive reply rate and meetings booked.
  • A message wins only if its positive reply rate is 30% or more above the other, and meetings booked do not point the other way. If neither is ahead when the test closes, it is a tie: we keep the simpler message and test a new one against it in month four.
  • Volumes are small, so every win this quarter is provisional. Outbound reaches 300 contacts a week, which is 50 a week for each of the six test cells. At the planned 1.5% positive reply rate, 150 contacts give about 2 replies a side and six weeks give about 4 or 5. A firm win needs about 10 interested replies a side, about 670 people a message, which this quarter cannot reach. So every win is used for now and tested again in month four with fresh contacts, and a gap of one reply counts as a tie.

The math: 150 × 1.5% = 2.25 replies. Six weeks at 50 contacts a week is 300 contacts a side, and 300 × 1.5% = 4.5 replies. Tests this small only find large differences, which is why the bar is a 30% gap read on replies and meetings together, and why a small win is checked again.

Who to contact first

At the same time, the demand generation lead tests the order of contact by group. Half of the groups in each size band start with a clinical contact, and the other half start with an executive. If not, both orders stay, and we read pilots started per 100 groups at Day 180.

Handing leads to sales

A lead gets a sales call after three checks, and hears back within an hour.

That keeps Tanner's calendar for people who can buy, and gets to them while they are still interested.

1 hour the longest a waits for a reply in business hours

The form shows a calendar as soon as it is sent, so a lead who books there waits no time at all.

What makes a lead sales-ready, and a call qualified

  1. Works at a target group: a physical therapy group with 5 to 25 clinics, or a pro or college sports program, in a state we sell into.
  2. Can approve a purchase or would use the product: one of the seven roles in the buying group.
  3. Asked for a demo, a pilot or pricing, or replied to our email with interest.
  4. Named a problem we help with, in their own words: return-to-sport decisions, patients who stall or drop out, or a new service to offer.
  5. Could start a 30-day pilot within 60 days.

A lead that passes checks 1 to 3 is sales-ready and goes on Tanner's calendar. The call counts as a qualified call once sales confirms checks 4 and 5 on it: the discovery questions for that role, under Learn more in the buying group section, draw out the problem, and the rep asks whether a 30-day pilot could start in the next 60 days. Leads from outside the target groups go to the weekly live demo and the follow-up emails.

How fast we reply

  • 0 minThe lead picks a time on a calendar shown right after the form.
  • 15 minA call or email if they did not book, in business hours.
  • 1 hrNo lead waits longer. Anything older shows in red on the weekly report.
  • 1 daySales accepts or rejects each lead, with a reason picked from a list.
  • 6 triesSales tries six times over ten business days. With no answer, the lead goes back to the follow-up emails.

How the objection notes are used

The demand generation lead and the sales lead review the objection field by role every Monday. Any objection heard three or more times without a good answer gets a draft answer that week. On the first Monday of each month, the answers and proof assets in the playbook are updated, and anything that changes a claim goes to regulatory first.

When to add a second inside-sales seat

The sales lead asks for a second inside-sales seat when either of these holds for two weeks in a row: the next open call slot is more than five business days out, or fewer than 80% of leads are answered within an hour. Leadership decides within a week of the ask and reviews it again at Day 90.
