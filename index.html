90-day demand generation plan

How to put 20 qualified discovery calls a week on the NeuroStrip sales calendar, what it costs and what it returns.

Kellen Levy · For the Control Bionics leadership team · October 2026

1Summary

The goal is 20 qualified discovery calls a week on the NeuroStrip sales calendar, with every call traced to the audience, message and channel that produced it.

Why PT groups first

  1. They can fill the calendar. 20 calls a week is about 1,000 conversations a year. There are about 1,000 PT groups with 5 to 25 locations, running about 10,000 clinics between them. US pro teams and Division I programs together number fewer than 600.
  2. One sale can grow to 25 sites. A group that pilots at one clinic can add its other locations on the same contract.
  3. The buyer is within reach. In a group this size a clinician usually sits on the leadership team. Above 50 locations, purchases go through committees and take quarters. Below 5, the deal is small and buyers compare us with a $499 biofeedback unit.

3What we say and offer

Every message makes one point. Strength and hop tests measure how much force a limb produces. NeuroStrip adds a live view of which muscles are producing it.

Positioning

For clinicians and performance staff who decide when a patient or athlete is ready, NeuroStrip is a wearable sensor that shows which muscles are doing the work, live, next to the strength tests they already use.

The account-based test

The test gets 45% of channel spend, which is $22,500 at the base budget, and runs for 90 days.

StepWhat happens
Build the listA purchased list from a healthcare practitioner database, HealthLink Dimensions or CampaignLake, both of which use NPI data. It is filtered to physical therapy practices by NPI record, to the states Control Bionics sells into, and to titles such as Clinic Owner, CEO, COO, Chief Clinical Officer, Rehab Director, Practice Administrator and Clinic Director. Neither site shows a filter for number of locations, so we keep practices with 5 to 25 locations by counting each group's practice addresses. CampaignLake lists a physical therapist segment and HealthLink Dimensions does not name one, so we check both free samples in week one.
Flag in-market practicesBombora flags practices whose research on topics such as physical therapy billing software, EMR migration, rehab equipment and biofeedback has risen above their own usual level. These in-market practices get ad spend and outbound first. Bombora reports research by company and does not name people, and the exact topics are checked against its topic list in week one.
Run the adsPropensity runs the ads. It describes itself as a contact-level B2B marketing platform. It builds audiences by industry, job role, tech stack and behavior, runs ads on LinkedIn, Meta, the Propensity Display Network, native and contextual display, direct mail and geofencing, and syncs contact-level engagement into Salesforce. We upload our own list to it. Its pricing page lists the plan that includes LinkedIn, Meta and direct mail at $3,000 a month, before ad spend.
Split the listTarget accounts are split at random into two halves. The test half gets ads plus outbound, and the holdout half gets outbound only. The split is made separately for in-market and not-yet-in-market practices, so both halves have the same mix.
Day 30At least 60% of target accounts must match to a verified decision maker. If they do not, the money moves to search, email and CSM.
Day 60The test half must reply to outbound at least 20% more often than the holdout half, or book more qualified calls per 100 accounts. If it does neither, the ads stop.
Month threeSpend shifts to in-market accounts, and a geofence runs around APTA CSM in Philadelphia, February 4 to 6, 2027.
FallbackIf Propensity cannot match enough of the list to run ads, the test moves to an NPI-based healthcare ad platform, PulsePoint or DeepIntent. Both target and report on healthcare professionals by individual NPI, and both accept an uploaded NPI list. They reach owners and clinic directors who hold an NPI, but not executives or administrators without one. Neither site names physical therapists, so our NPI list gets a match test first. Neither publishes prices, so both are quotes in week one.

5Budget

The base case is $90,000 over 90 days. 55% goes to media, 37% to content and setup, and 8% stays in reserve until the Day 30 and Day 60 reviews.

What this budget returns

    Line items

    Spend by month

    • Month 1 is mostly content and setup.
    • Media spend about doubles in month 2 as the tests go live.
    • In month 3 the reserve goes to whatever is working, so most of the month is media.

    Where the calls come from at Day 90

      Planning rates: 55% of qualified calls become sales opportunities, 45% of those start a pilot, and half of pilots convert, at $7,500 in first-year contract value. Search is planned at $330 per qualified call, from published 2026 benchmarks. The account-based test keeps the $2,000 per call that was planned for LinkedIn until its own results replace it. Propensity lists the plan that includes LinkedIn, Meta and direct mail at $3,000 a month. Over 90 days that fee takes $9,000 of the account-based line, which leaves about $13,500 for ads at the base budget and about $2,250 at Lean. The list and intent data have no published prices and are quoted in week one. Only search and the account-based test change with the budget here. Outbound, the weekly demo, CSM and existing inbound stay constant, which is why the range between Lean and Aggressive is narrow. Real numbers from the last 12 months of trials replace these rates by Day 30.

      6The first 90 days

      We spend month one setting up tracking and content, month two testing messages and offers, and month three putting money behind what worked.

      Dates assume Day 1 is December 1, 2026. Full launch then lands in the first week of January, after the holidays, and APTA CSM falls in week 10.

      Days 1 to 30

      Set up

      1. Week 1Vendor demos and coverage counts. Propensity, HealthLink Dimensions, CampaignLake and Bombora each count the PT practices with 5 to 25 locations they cover in our states. Confirm email and ad use rights before signing. Listen to ten sales calls, interview trial customers, and agree the qualified-lead checklist with sales.
      2. Week 2Buy the list and load it into Salesforce with NPI, data source and intent fields. Salesforce stages, source tracking and lead routing go live. Launch Google Search. Draft the approved claims list with regulatory and book two customer video shoots.
      3. Week 3Build the Propensity audience, then split target accounts at random into a test half and a holdout half. If Propensity matches too little of the list, move the test to PulsePoint or DeepIntent. Build the four landing pages, produce the 90-second video and a pilot guide, and re-brief the outbound partner.
      4. Week 4Launch the account-based ads to the test half at low spend. Run the first weekly live demo. The weekly report goes live, and a test lead runs through every path.

      Day 30 checkpoint

      6 to 8 qualified calls a week

      At least 60% of target accounts match to a verified decision maker, or the account-based money moves to search, email and CSM. Decide: pick the two messages to test and approve month-two spend.

      Days 31 to 60

      Test

      1. Week 5Full launch. Outbound starts to both halves, in-market practices first, and two messages are tested against two buyer types.
      2. Week 6Make the first cut of ads that are not working. Add new keywords and exclusions from the search terms report. Open meeting bookings for CSM.
      3. Week 7Test the offers: pilot scoping call against live demo. Publish the first customer story.
      4. Week 8Cut the bottom half of ads and audiences. Choose the winning message and offer.

      Day 60 checkpoint

      10 to 12 qualified calls a week

      The test half replies to outbound at least 20% more often than the holdout half, or books more qualified calls per 100 accounts. If neither, the ads stop. Decide: release the reserve to what is working.

      Days 61 to 90

      Scale

      1. Week 9Move ad spend to in-market practices. Raise spend on what works by up to 25% a week. Widen outbound if reply rates hold.
      2. Week 10APTA CSM runs February 4 to 6 in Philadelphia, with a geofence around the convention center. Hold the pre-booked meetings, run the booth muscle test, and get every badge scan into Salesforce the same day.
      3. Week 11Follow up every CSM conversation within 48 hours. Publish the second customer story. First newsletter sponsorship goes live.
      4. Weeks 12 and 13Read the first pilot results against their success measures. Hold the Day 90 review of cost per call, per pilot and per customer, by channel, audience and test half.

      Day 90 checkpoint

      15 to 20 qualified calls a week

      About 130 qualified calls held in total and 30 pilots started or scheduled. Decide: next quarter's budget, whether to renew the 90-day data and ABM contracts, a second sales seat, and the second audience to target.

      7Handing leads to sales

      Sales gets leads that meet five tests, and every one of them hears from us within an hour.

      What counts as a qualified lead

      1. Works at a target account: a PT group with 5 to 25 locations, or a pro or college sports program, in a region we sell to.
      2. Can approve a purchase or would use the product: an owner or executive, or a clinical or performance lead.
      3. Asked for something: a demo, a pilot or pricing, or replied to outbound.
      4. Named a problem we solve, in their own words: return-to-sport decisions, patients who plateau or drop out, or a new service to sell.
      5. Could start a 30-day pilot within 60 days.

      Points 1 to 3 come from the form and Salesforce and put the lead straight on the sales calendar. Sales confirms points 4 and 5 on the call. Leads that miss point 1 or 2 go to the weekly group demo and email instead.

      Response times

      • 0 minQualified leads pick a time on a calendar shown right after they submit the form.
      • 15 minFirst call or email if they did not book, during business hours.
      • 1 hrNo lead waits longer than this. Anything older shows in red on the weekly report.
      • 1 daySales accepts or rejects each lead, with a reason from a fixed list.
      • 6 touchesOver ten business days before an unresponsive lead goes back to email nurture.

      Salesforce in month one

      Week 1

      • Audit fields, the Account Engagement connection and admin access
      • NeuroStrip record types, kept apart from assistive communication
      • Patient records excluded from every marketing list and report
      • Fields for account type, size and role, plus NPI, data source, intent topic, and test or holdout group

      Week 2

      • Lifecycle stages, each with a date stamp
      • Lead source and UTM fields that cannot be overwritten
      • "How did you hear about us?" on every form
      • Lead routing with the response-time clock

      Week 3

      • Parent accounts for groups, child accounts for clinics
      • Opportunity stages with pilot start, end and success measures
      • Required loss reasons from a fixed list
      • Simple fit and engagement scores

      Week 4

      • The weekly report
      • The last 12 months of trials entered with source and outcome
      • Qualified stages sent back to Google Ads
      • Propensity engagement synced to each contact
      • A test lead through every path, and 30 minutes of training for sales

      Five numbers reported every week

      1. Qualified discovery calls heldagainst the goal of 20
      2. Leads answered within an houras a share of all qualified leads
      3. Calls that became sales opportunitiesplanned at 55%
      4. Pilots started, and pilots converted to paidplanned at 45% and 50%
      5. Cost per qualified callby channel, against a limit of about $930

      Each number is split by channel, by audience and by test half. The $930 limit is a $7,500 first-year contract times the 12% of qualified calls planned to become customers.
