The first 90 days buy three things: a wired revenue system, a message and offer that have earned their budget, and unit economics by source. The next 90 spend that knowledge.

Why this plan is built the way it is

Seven things a typical 90-day plan does not do.

  1. It works backward from the calendar.The plan starts at 20 calls a week and the constraints around them, not at a list of channels.
  2. It names the market's real size.About 1,000 accounts is an account-based problem, and the plan says so.
  3. It labels every claim.Verified, shared, assumed or hypothesized, with the source one tap away.
  4. It lets you move the numbers.Change the weights, the budget or the rates and watch what holds.
  5. It uses what already exists.A year of trials, what looks like an engagement platform already in place, an outbound program, named reference customers, an event inside the window.
  6. It writes the kill rules first.Before a dollar is spent, there is an answer for what happens if it does not work.
  7. It is the work sample.Researched, modeled, designed and built the way the job would be done.

The first 30 days are the cheapest part of this plan and the part everything else rests on.

I would start with the audit and ten customer conversations.
