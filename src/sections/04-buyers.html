The three tests

How each test runs

  • Within each cluster, contacts are randomly assigned to message A or message B through the Propensity ad audiences and the outbound email sequences.
  • We read each test at about 150 contacts per side in week 8. If neither message leads by 30%, the test runs to six weeks, about 300 contacts a side, and closes by February 15, the end of week 11.
  • We track two metrics: positive reply rate and meetings booked.
  • A message wins only if its positive reply rate is at least 30% above the other's and meetings booked do not point the other way. If neither leads when the test closes, we record a tie, keep the simpler message and test a new challenger against it in month four.
  • Volumes are small, so every win this quarter is provisional. Outbound reaches 300 contacts a week, which is 50 a week for each of the six test cells. At the planned 1.5% positive reply rate, 150 contacts yield about 2 replies a side, and six weeks yield about 4 or 5. A reliable win needs about 10 interested replies a side, or about 670 people per message, which this quarter cannot reach. We therefore apply each win provisionally, retest it in month four with fresh contacts, and treat a gap of one reply as a tie.

The calculation is 150 × 1.5% = 2.25 replies. Six weeks at 50 contacts a week gives 300 contacts a side, and 300 × 1.5% = 4.5 replies. Tests this small detect only large differences, which is why the bar is a 30% gap read on replies and meetings together, and why we recheck any small win.

Which contact comes first

In parallel, the demand generation lead tests contact order by group. Half of the groups in each size band start with a clinical contact, and the other half start with an executive. At Day 90, we compare qualified calls per 100 groups in each half. If one order leads by at least 30%, it becomes the default for next quarter's new groups. If neither does, both orders continue, and we compare pilots started per 100 groups at Day 180.

What sales records on every call
