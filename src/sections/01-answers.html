The brief asked five things. Here they are without the wind-up. Everything after this section is the reasoning, the machinery and the evidence behind each answer.

Q1

Which audience comes first: sports performance, physical therapy, or both?

Physical therapy first. Sport proves it.

70% 20% 10%

Regional PT groupsSport, the proof laneInbound capture

  • Volume engine: outpatient groups with 5 to 25 locations. It is the only segment big enough to feed 20 calls a week, and one clinic can become 25.
  • Proof lane: elite and college sport. Business development hunts the logos; demand gen supplies air cover and content.
  • Capture only: independents, research, performance facilities. No patient or consumer spend.

Q3

How should an agency be used, and what stays in-house?

Own the brain and the data. Rent the hands.

  • In-house: strategy, the account list, messaging, offers, test rules, ad accounts, reporting, and media buying for the first 90 days.
  • Partners: proof production, outbound execution, a fixed four-week Salesforce sprint.
  • No media agency yet. Revisit past roughly $40K a month.
The operating model {{i:arrow-right}}
Q4

What is a qualified lead, and how is performance measured through revenue?

A right-fit account with a named problem and a path to a pilot.

  • Fit times intent decides routing. Fit A or B with a hand-raise books straight onto the calendar.
  • Sales qualifies with PILOT: Problem, ICP, Leader, Operator, Timing.
  • Scorecard: held calls, sales-accepted rate, pilot starts, pilot to paid, cost per each, by source.
The qualification model {{i:arrow-right}}
Q5

What gets implemented in Salesforce in month one?

Wiring first. Dashboards second.

  • Structure: NeuroStrip record types, lifecycle stages with timestamps, source capture that survives, a campaign hierarchy that mirrors the reporting question.
  • Motion: routing with a response SLA, pilot-aware opportunity stages, required loss reasons.
  • Head start: backfill twelve months of trials for baselines on day 30.
