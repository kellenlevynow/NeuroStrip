Physical therapy groups with 5 to 25 clinics

We target a purchased list of about 1,000 groups. Paid media, search and email reach their owners, leadership teams and clinicians. The conversion path runs from a call to a 30-day pilot at one clinic, then expands to additional clinics.

20% of team time and attention

Pro and college sport

Control Bionics' business development lead sells directly to each team. Marketing supplies return-to-sport content (material on bringing injured athletes back to play), ads targeted to a short list of named programs, and the event plan. The goal is sport customer stories that, once cleared, build credibility with physical therapy groups.

10% of team time and attention

All other buyers

Single clinics outside a group, training facilities and researchers find us on their own through search, the website and a weekly live demo. We send them no unsolicited email and run no ads aimed at patients or the public.

The decision maker is reachable.

In a group this size, a therapist usually sits on the leadership team, so the person who sees the clinical value can help approve the purchase. Above 50 clinics, purchasing runs through committees and takes months. Below 5 clinics, deals are small and buyers compare NeuroStrip with a $499 unit that reads one muscle.2

How large the segment is

The United States has about 39,000 outpatient physical therapy clinics, and about 82% belong to independents and groups with fewer than 25 clinics. About 10,000 clinics sit in groups with 5 to 25 clinics, a planning estimate we check against the purchased list in week one. At an average of 6 to 15 clinics per group, that implies roughly 700 to 1,700 groups. The plan assumes about 1,000 and confirms the actual number from the purchased list in week one.3

Why sport receives 20%

Sport teams decide quickly and understand muscle testing, so the demo resonates fast. However, there are too few teams to fill 20 calls a week, their budgets reset once a year, and Control Bionics has two open roles designed for exactly this split: business development for named sport programs, and demand generation for volume. Sport stories, once cleared, then support sales to physical therapy groups.

What would change the priority order

If the week-one count shows far fewer than 700 groups, we add groups with 26 to 50 clinics before adding new audiences. If Propensity matches too little of the list in week 3, we test PulsePoint or DeepIntent. If fewer than 60% of groups are matched by Day 30, we shift the ad budget to search, email and the conference and add groups with 26 to 50 clinics to the list.
