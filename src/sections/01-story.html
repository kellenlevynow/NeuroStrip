Follow one group

Winning one 12-clinic group takes about 13 weeks and $4,100 of marketing.

Here is how one physical therapy (PT) group, a company that owns 12 clinics, moves through the plan, and what each step costs. The rest of the page explains the system behind it.

$4,100 of marketing to win one new customer, a group that signs for its first clinic, at the recommended $90,000 budget

We plan on one clinic paying Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, about $625 a month, so the $4,100 is earned back in about seven months.1

  1. Week 1

    The group is on our list.

    We buy a list from a database of healthcare providers. It finds the group's clinics by their and shows that all 12 share one owner. It names the owner, the chief operating officer (COO), the rehabilitation (rehab) director who leads the group's therapy work, and the directors of four of the clinics.

    Cost: about $11, this group's share of what we pay for the list and the research data

  2. Week 3

    People there start researching.

    Bombora, an company, shows that staff at the group are reading more than usual about rehab equipment and billing software. Bombora reports by company and does not name people. That marks the group as , so it moves to the front of the line for ads and email.

    Cost: already counted in the $11 above

  3. Week 4

    Its leaders start to see our ads.

    The list is split at random into two halves. This group lands in the half that sees , bought through Propensity, an account-based ad platform. The other half, the , gets the same emails without ads, so we can measure what the ads add. The owner sees a 15-second video on LinkedIn. The clinic directors see banner ads on physical therapy news sites.

    Cost: about $45 of ads over 90 days

  4. Week 5

    The rehab director gets an email. The owner gets a box.

    The rehab director receives the first of four short, plain emails. The owner receives a box with a sample strip on a card and a one-page economics worksheet built from the group's own visit numbers.

    Cost: about $5 of email. The box price is a quote in week one.

  5. Week 6

    The rehab director books a call.

    The rehab director replies and books a time on the calendar of Tanner, the inside-sales rep who takes these calls. The lead passes our five checks, so the call counts as one , the number this whole plan is built to grow.

    Cost: about $510 of marketing per qualified call in month three, about half the $930 a call can cost and still pay back in its first year

  6. Week 7

    They agree to a 30-day pilot.

    On the call, the rehab director, the owner and one clinic director agree to a , a 30-day trial at one clinic. Before it starts, they write their own success measures, such as minutes added to a visit and how often therapists use it.

    Cost: sales time, outside this budget

  7. Weeks 8 to 12

    One clinic runs the pilot.

    The clinic director uses NeuroStrip with real patients for 30 days. The rehab director checks the results against the measures they wrote.

    Cost: one kit on loan from the set Control Bionics keeps for pilots, plus setup time, both outside this budget. Pilot terms: confirm in week one.

  8. Week 13

    The owner signs for the first clinic.

    The pilot met its measures, so the owner signs. That clinic is planned to pay Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, more than the $4,100 it took to win.3

    Value: about $7,500 in the first year

  9. Months 4 to 12

    The group rolls it out to more clinics.

    The COO plans the next clinics on the same contract. If each extra clinic were priced like the first, all 12 would bring in about $90,000 a year with no new marketing spend. The price per extra clinic is confirmed in week one.

    Value: up to about $90,000 a year across 12 clinics

Where the $4,100 comes from

At the base budget, month three spends about $33,300 and produces about 15 qualified calls a week, or about 65 a month. That is about $510 per qualified call. Of those calls, 55% become real sales opportunities, 45% of those start a pilot, and half of pilots sign. So about 12.4% of qualified calls become customers, and $510 divided by 0.124 is about $4,100 per new customer.

How the per-group costs are worked out

  • List and data: the $10,800 data line spread over about 1,000 target groups is about $11 a group. It also pays for scheduling tools and Salesforce work.
  • Ads: the $22,500 account-based line, including Propensity's listed fee, spread over the roughly 500 groups in the half that sees ads, is about $45 a group.
  • Email: the $5,400 email line spread over all 1,000 groups is about $5 a group.
  • The direct mail box has no published price. It is quoted in week one and capped inside the small-tests line.

What would change this story

Three numbers matter most: how many qualified calls become customers, the real first-year value, and the real price per extra clinic. All three come from Control Bionics' last 12 months of trials, which we enter in Salesforce in month one. By Day 30 these planning figures are replaced with real ones.

  1. $4,100 is the base budget's month-three cost per qualified call ($510) divided by the share of calls planned to become customers (12.4%). Seven months is $4,100 divided by $7,500 a year, or $625 a month.
  2. The 7% and 37% figures are one athlete's example from Control Bionics: three weeks after a hamstring injury, force plates showed a 7% gap between legs and NeuroStrip showed a 37% gap in muscle activation. NeuroStrip is a non-diagnostic measurement and biofeedback tool.
  3. $7,500 is a planning figure for first-year value, replaced by real contract values from the trial history in month one. Price per extra clinic is confirmed in week one.
