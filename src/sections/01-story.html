Follow one group

Signing a 12-clinic group's first clinic takes about 13 weeks and, on average, about $6,900 of marketing.

The example below follows a made-up physical therapy (PT) company that owns 12 clinics through each stage of the plan and shows what every step costs at the plan's planning figures. The rest of the page explains the system behind it.

$6,900 in average marketing spend to acquire one new customer from the calls the recommended $90,000 budget funds

The plan assumes one clinic pays Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, or about $625 a month, so about 11 months of that clinic's payments recover the $6,900, before product, sales and pilot kit costs.1

    The group enters our target list.

    We purchase a list from a healthcare provider database that identifies clinics by their . Matching practice addresses rolls the 12 clinics up under one owner. The list names the leaders it covers, such as the owner and the clinical director who leads the group's therapy work. We confirm in week one which job titles each list covers.

    The group begins actively researching solutions.

    Bombora, an provider, reports that staff at the group are reading more than usual about rehab equipment and billing software. Bombora reports at the company level and does not identify individuals. That signal flags the group as , so it moves to the top of the priority queue for ads and email.

    Cost: included in the $11 above

  3. Week 4

    The group's leaders begin seeing our ads.

    We split the list at random into two halves. This group falls in the half that receives , purchased through Propensity, an account-based advertising platform. The other half, the , receives the same emails without ads, which lets us measure the incremental lift the ads deliver. The owner sees a five-card carousel ad on LinkedIn. The clinic directors see the 15-second video and banner ads on websites in Propensity's network.

    Cost: about $45 over 90 days, comprising about $27 of media plus this group's share of Propensity's fee

  4. Week 5

    The clinical director and the owner each receive an email.

    The clinical director receives the first of four short, plainly written emails, and the owner receives the first email in the owner sequence. If the owner had not replied by week 8, a direct mail box would follow, containing a sample strip and a one-page estimate built on labeled planning figures that the group can replace with its own.

    Cost: about $5 of email. Box price: quote in week one.

  5. Week 6

    The clinical director books a call.

    The clinical director replies and books time on the calendar of Tanner, the inside-sales representative who runs these calls. The lead meets all five of our qualification criteria, so the call counts as one , the metric this plan is built to grow.

    Cost: about $860 of marketing per budget-funded qualified call in month three, just under the $930 a call can cost and still be covered by first-year payments

  6. Week 7

    The group agrees to a 30-day pilot.

    On the call, the clinical director, the owner and one clinic director agree to a , a 30-day trial at one clinic. Before it begins, they define their own success measures, such as minutes added to a visit and how often therapists use the device.

    Cost: sales time, outside this budget

  7. Weeks 8 to 12

    One clinic runs the pilot.

    The clinic director uses NeuroStrip with real patients for 30 days. The clinical director then evaluates the results against the success measures the group defined.

    Cost: one kit on loan from the set Control Bionics keeps for pilots, plus setup time, both outside this budget. Pilot terms: confirm in week one.

  8. Week 13

    The owner signs for the first clinic.

    The pilot meets its success measures, so the owner signs. The plan assumes that clinic pays Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, more than the $6,900 it cost, on average, to acquire.3

    Value: about $7,500 in the first year

  9. Months 4 to 12

    The group extends NeuroStrip to more clinics.

    The group's chief operating officer (COO) plans the next clinics under the same contract. If each extra clinic were priced like the first, all 12 would generate about $90,000 a year with no new marketing spend. Price per extra clinic: confirm in week one.

    Value: up to about $90,000 a year across 12 clinics

How the $6,900 is derived

At the base budget, month-three spend is about $33,300. The plan expects about 15 qualified calls a week at Day 90, but about 4 of those already arrive each week today, and about 2 come from the February conference, which has its own budget. The spend funds the remaining 9 or so a week, about 39 a month, so each costs about $860. Of qualified calls, 55% become sales opportunities, 45% of those start a pilot, and half of pilots sign. Overall, about 12.4% of calls convert to customers, and $860 divided by 0.124 is about $6,900 per new customer.

Across every call, including those this budget does not fund, the same month costs about $510 per call and $4,100 per customer. We use the higher figures because incremental spend only buys the calls it funds.

This group's own share is about $61 plus the box. The $6,900 also covers the groups that never buy, because about 8 qualified calls lead to each customer.

How the per-group costs are calculated

  • List and data: the $10,800 data line spread over about 1,000 target groups is about $11 a group. It also funds scheduling tools and Salesforce work.
  • Ads: the $22,500 account-based line spread over the roughly 500 groups in the half that sees ads is about $45 a group, comprising about $27 of media plus this group's share of Propensity's $9,000 fee.
  • Email: the $5,400 email line spread over all 1,000 groups is about $5 a group.
  • The direct mail box has no published price. Its price is a quote in week one, capped within the small-tests line.

What would change this example

Three inputs matter most: the share of qualified calls that become customers, the actual first-year value, and the actual price per extra clinic. All three come from Control Bionics' last 12 months of trials, which we load into Salesforce in month one. By Day 30, actual figures replace these planning figures.
