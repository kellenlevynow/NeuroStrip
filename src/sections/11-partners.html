11Agency and in-house {{i:circle-help}}Answers question 3

Own the brain and the data. Rent the hands.

The role description already says it: "manage external creative and marketing partners to support campaign execution."{{ev:v:s14}} The question is which work to hand over. My answer: anything where the learning is the asset stays inside, and anything where the craft is specialized and the demand is lumpy goes out.

{{i:brain}}

Stays in-house

The thinking and the truth

  • Strategy, ICP and the account listIt is the asset. An agency that owns the list owns the business.
  • Positioning, messaging and offersThey come from customer conversations that sales and marketing sit in.
  • Experiment design and decision rulesWhoever writes the rules owns the learning.
  • Ad accounts, pixels, audiences and dataAlways in Control Bionics' name. Leaving a partner should never mean losing history.
  • Media buying, for the first 90 daysThe spend is small and what it teaches is the product.
  • Reporting definitions and the weekly sales loopOne version of what "qualified" means.
  • Claims approvalWith regulatory, before anything ships.

{{i:ban}}No media agency yet

At this spend the fees are not justified, and in a market of 1,000 accounts the weekly search-terms report and LinkedIn account report are the most valuable reading in the building. They should be read by the person writing next week's tests. Revisit when either line is crossed:

  • $40Kof media a month
  • 4+paid channels live

{{i:scale}}Four rules for any partner

  1. Control Bionics owns everything. Accounts, pixels, audiences, source files, data.
  2. The scorecard is sales-accepted calls. Not clicks, not leads, not impressions.
  3. No percentage-of-spend fees. Nobody should earn more because the budget went up.
  4. Thirty-day exit. A partner that performs does not need a lock-in.

First partner conversation

Re-briefing the outbound program.

Outbound is the cheapest route to a meeting and the control arm for the LinkedIn test, so it gets attention in week one. Six changes turn a volume program into a coordinated one.{{ev:a:a11}}

  1. Tiers, not volume. Tier A named accounts first, with the signals attached.
  2. A message matrix. Sequences map to the experiments, one variable at a time.
  3. Every touch logged. Activity and campaign membership in Salesforce, same day.
  4. Shared suppression and holdout lists. So the air-cover test stays clean.
  5. Quality over count. Judged, and where possible paid, on held Tier A and B meetings.
  6. Domain health, weekly. Separate sending domains, bounce and complaint lines that pause sending.
