09Lead qualification {{i:circle-help}}Answers question 4

A qualified lead is a right-fit account with a named problem and a path to a pilot.

The definition has to be simple enough that sales uses it and strict enough that the calendar is protected. Two questions decide where a lead goes. Five decide whether a call became pipeline.

  1. 1

    Inquiry

    Any identified person who engages in a tracked way: a form, a reply, a registration, a badge scan.

    System
  2. 2

    Lead

    A real person in a business context, with segment and tier assigned.

    Marketing
  3. 3

    Engaged lead

    Meaningful activity in the last 30 days: demo watched, live demo attended, pilot or pricing page viewed.

    Marketing
  4. 4

    MQL

    Fit A or B plus a hand-raise, or Fit A plus sustained engagement. Routed with a response SLA.

    Hand-off
  5. 5

    SAL

    Inside sales accepts within one business day, or rejects with a reason from a fixed list.

  6. 6

    Discovery call held and the PILOT checklist met. Converts to an opportunity.

Marketing's side · two questions, not twenty

Fit times intent decides the route.

Fit comes from who they are. Intent comes from what they did. Account Engagement already has both built in as a grade and a score, so nothing new has to be invented.{{ev:a:a10}} Try a lead.

Sales' side · five questions after the call

PILOT. Four of five, and the first two are not optional.

Most engagements so far began as trials{{ev:v:s9}}, and some have converted to revenue.{{ev:s:s15}} So the test of a qualified opportunity is whether a pilot can start, not whether a budget line exists. The plan bets that a structured 30-day pilot converts at half or better.{{ev:h:h7}}

  1. P

    Problem

    They named a specific problem in their own words: return-to-sport decisions, plateaued patients, drop-off, a program to sell.

    Mandatory
  2. I

    ICP

    Segment and size put the account in Tier A or B, in a region we can serve.

    Mandatory
  3. L

    Leader

    The person who can approve a pilot is identified and knows the conversation is happening.

  4. O

    Operator

    A named clinician or staff member will run it, at a named site.

  5. T

    Timing

    A pilot could start inside 60 days. For teams, the season allows it.

{{i:send}}

What sales receives with every MQL

  • Segment, number of locations, tier and persona
  • The campaign and message that produced the hand-raise
  • The last three things they did, with dates
  • Who else at the account has engaged
  • Their own words from the form: the problem, and how they heard of us
{{i:inbox}}

What marketing gets back

  • Accepted or rejected within one business day, with a reason
  • Call held, no-show or rescheduled
  • The five PILOT answers on the opportunity
  • Pilot start, end and outcome against criteria
  • A loss reason from a fixed list, never "other"

Response SLA

The fastest reply is the one the buyer books themselves.

Research on online leads has found most companies respond far too slowly.{{ev:v:s39}} Tier A and B hand-raisers see the calendar on the thank-you page. Nobody waits for an email.

  • 0minCalendar shown on the thank-you page for Fit A and B hand-raisers
  • 15minTarget for a first call or email if they did not book, in business hours
  • 1hrHard ceiling. Past it, the lead shows red on the speed-to-lead dashboard
  • 6×Touches over ten business days before a lead recycles to nurture

Capacity guard

One calendar holds 20 to 25 calls a week.{{ev:a:a4}}

Tier A gets 1:1 time. Tier B gets the group demo first. A second inside-sales seat is triggered, in writing, when either holds for two straight weeks:

  • The next open slot is more than five business days out
  • SLA attainment falls under 80%
