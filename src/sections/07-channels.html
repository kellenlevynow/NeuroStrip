Channels

Three channels command three quarters of the paid media budget.

The three are account-based ads to a named list of practice groups, Google Search and email. Together they are planned to deliver about 7 of the 15 qualified calls a week at Day 90. Each channel has a performance threshold that sustains its funding and a defined point at which it is cut.

Account-based ads

Ads reach the owners, leaders and clinicians at half of the target list. The other half, the , receives email only, which isolates the incremental effect of the ads. Propensity's fee of $3,000 a month is funded from this line, leaving about $13,500 for ad spend.

The plan assumes $2,000 per qualified call all-in, more than twice the $930 limit, so this line is funded as a 90-day test. Retains its funding if it passes two checks. At Day 60, the test half must not trail the holdout half on both interested replies and calls per 100 groups. At Day 90, it must book at least 20% more qualified calls per 100 groups, at under $930 a call. Cut if, at Day 30, Propensity can match fewer than 60% of groups to a named decision maker. The budget is then reallocated to search, email and the conference.

Google Search

Ads appear when a prospect searches for muscle sensors, biofeedback, a competitor or help with return-to-sport decisions. The campaign goes live in week two.

Retains its funding if sales agrees that at least 35 of every 100 search leads are worth a call. Cut if a keyword group spends $400, above the planned $330 cost per qualified call from search, without producing a lead from a target group.

11% · $5,400

Email

Every contact on the list receives four short, plain-text emails, with groups researching now sequenced first. Contacts who download an asset enter the follow-up series for their role.

Retains its funding if 1.5% or more of recipients reply with interest, about 4 or 5 replies per 300 people emailed, which is the rate the plan assumes. Rewritten if a series earns interested replies from under 1% of its first 300 recipients.

StepWhat happens
Build the list
Weeks 1 and 2		We purchase a list from a healthcare provider database, either HealthLink Dimensions or CampaignLake. Both use records. The list is filtered to physical therapy practices in the states Control Bionics sells into and to the seven titles in the buying group. Neither vendor's site shows a filter for number of clinics, so we identify groups with 5 to 25 clinics by counting each group's practice addresses. CampaignLake lists a physical therapist segment and HealthLink Dimensions does not name one, so we review both free samples in week one.
Flag groups researching now
Weekly from week 2		Bombora identifies which groups are reading more than usual about topics such as physical therapy billing software, electronic medical record (EMR) migration, rehab equipment and biofeedback. Those groups receive ads and email first. Bombora reports at the company level and does not identify individuals. The exact topic names are verified against Bombora's topic list in week one.
Run the ads
From week 4		Propensity runs the ads. It describes itself as a contact-level business-to-business marketing platform. It builds audiences by industry, job role, tech stack and behavior, runs ads on LinkedIn, Meta, the Propensity Display Network, native and contextual display, direct mail and geofencing, and sends contact-level engagement into Salesforce. We upload our own list to the platform. Its pricing page (propensity.com/pricing, viewed October 2026) lists the plan that includes LinkedIn, Meta and direct mail at $3,000 a month, before ad spend. The required plan is confirmed in the week-one demo.

Week 3		Groups are randomly assigned to two halves of about 500 each. The test half receives ads plus email. The receives email only. The split is run separately for groups researching now and for all other groups, so both halves carry the same mix.
Day 30 check
December 30, 2026		At least 60% of target groups must match to a named, verified decision maker. If they do not, the ad budget is reallocated to search, email and the APTA Combined Sections Meeting (CSM), and groups with 26 to 50 clinics are added to the list.

January 29, 2027		The ads stop only if the test half is behind the holdout half on both interested replies and qualified calls per 100 groups. If the test half leads on both, the month-three increase proceeds. If it is level, or leads on only one, spend holds at the month-two level until Day 90.

From January 30, 2027		Ad spend shifts to groups researching now. A runs around APTA CSM in Philadelphia, February 4 to 6, 2027.
Fallback
Decided in week 3		By December 21, if Propensity matches fewer than 60% of groups to a named decision maker, the same list runs through a healthcare ad platform that targets by NPI, either PulsePoint or DeepIntent, and the test moves to whichever achieves the higher match rate. The Day 30 check then applies to the platform in use. Both platforms target and report on individual providers by NPI and accept an uploaded NPI list. They reach owners and clinic directors who hold an NPI. Executives without an NPI fall outside their reach. Neither site names physical therapists, so our list undergoes a match test first. Neither publishes prices, so pricing for both is a quote in week one.

How reliable the Day 60 read can be

Email starts in week 5, so by Day 60 it has reached about 1,350 contacts, about 675 in each half, and many of them are still partway through the four emails. At the planned 1.5% reply rate, that yields about 10 interested replies per half, so a 20% lift equals about 2 replies, a difference that chance alone can produce. The Day 60 read therefore confirms that nothing is broken and that the ads are not clearly worse. The full read comes at Day 90, once most of the list has been emailed, and it uses replies and qualified calls per 100 groups together.

The APTA Combined Sections Meeting in February

The American Physical Therapy Association's Combined Sections Meeting (APTA CSM) runs February 4 to 6, 2027, in Philadelphia. We book meetings with target practices before the show, run the geofence and a 60-second muscle test at the booth during it, and follow up on every conversation within 48 hours. Event costs are funded from a separate budget.

What the plan defers

The plan defers LinkedIn self-serve prospecting, which reaches this buyer poorly. It also defers broad Meta prospecting beyond the verified list, paid YouTube reach, content syndication, and any patient or consumer advertising.
