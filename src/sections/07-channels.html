Learn more about the account-based ad test

The test runs for 90 days on 45% of the ad and email money, $22,500 at the base budget. The owner of every step is the demand generation lead, with leadership deciding at Day 30 and Day 60.

StepWhat happens
Build the list
Weeks 1 and 2		We buy a list from a healthcare provider database, HealthLink Dimensions or CampaignLake. Both use records. We keep physical therapy practices in the states Control Bionics sells into, and the seven titles in the buying group. Neither site shows a filter for number of clinics, so we keep groups with 5 to 25 clinics by counting each group's practice addresses. CampaignLake lists a physical therapist segment and HealthLink Dimensions does not name one, so we check both free samples in week one.
Flag practices researching now
Weekly from week 2		Bombora shows which practices are reading more than usual about topics such as physical therapy billing software, electronic medical record (EMR) migration, rehab equipment and biofeedback. Those practices get ads and email first. Bombora reports by company and does not name people. The exact topic names are checked against Bombora's topic list in week one.
Run the ads
From week 4		Propensity runs the ads. It describes itself as a contact-level business-to-business marketing platform. It builds audiences by industry, job role, tech stack and behavior, runs ads on LinkedIn, Meta, the Propensity Display Network, native and contextual display, direct mail and geofencing, and sends contact-level engagement into Salesforce. We upload our own list to it. Its pricing page lists the plan that includes LinkedIn, Meta and direct mail at $3,000 a month, before ad spend.
Split the list
Week 3		Groups are split at random into two halves of about 500. The test half gets ads plus email. The gets email only. The split is made separately for practices researching now and the rest, so both halves have the same mix.
Day 30 check
December 30, 2026		At least 60% of target groups must match to a named, verified decision maker. If not, the ad money moves to search, email and the APTA Combined Sections Meeting (CSM).
Day 60 check
January 29, 2027		The test half must reply to email at least 20% more often than the holdout half, or book more qualified calls per 100 groups. If neither, the ads stop. If the result is close, we run 30 more days at the same spend before scaling.
Month three
From January 30, 2027		Ad spend moves to practices researching now. A runs around APTA CSM in Philadelphia, February 4 to 6, 2027.
Fallback
Decided in week 3		If Propensity cannot match enough of the list to run ads, the test moves to a healthcare ad platform that targets by NPI, PulsePoint or DeepIntent. Both target and report on individual providers by NPI and accept an uploaded NPI list. They reach owners and clinic directors who hold an NPI, but not executives without one. Neither site names physical therapists, so our list gets a match test first. Neither publishes prices, so both are quotes in week one.

How sure the Day 60 read can be

Each half has about 500 groups and roughly 1,000 to 1,500 contacts. At the planned 1.5% positive reply rate that is about 18 replies a half, so a 20% lift is about 4 extra replies, which chance alone can produce. That is why the rule reads replies and qualified calls together, and why a close result buys 30 more days instead of more budget.

The APTA Combined Sections Meeting in February

The American Physical Therapy Association's Combined Sections Meeting (APTA CSM) runs February 4 to 6, 2027, in Philadelphia. We book meetings with target practices before the show, run the geofence and a 60-second muscle test at the booth during it, and follow up every conversation within 48 hours. Event costs sit in their own budget.

What we are holding back on

LinkedIn self-serve prospecting, which reaches this buyer poorly. Also broad Meta prospecting beyond the verified list, paid YouTube reach, content syndication, and any patient or consumer advertising.
