**This 90-day plan builds toward 20 qualified sales calls a week.**

Prepared by Kellen Levy for the Control Bionics leadership team, October 2026. This is the text version of https://kellenlevynow.github.io/NeuroStrip/, generated from the page so the words match. It is an independent proposal. Control Bionics did not publish it.

## 1. Follow one group

**Signing a 12-clinic group's first clinic takes about 13 weeks and, on average, about $6,900 of marketing.**

The example below follows a made-up physical therapy (PT) company that owns 12 clinics through each stage of the plan and shows what every step costs at the plan's planning figures. The rest of the page explains the system behind it.

**$6,900** in average marketing spend to acquire one new customer from the calls the recommended $90,000 budget funds. The plan assumes one clinic pays Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, or about $625 a month, so about 11 months of that clinic's payments recover the $6,900, before product, sales and pilot kit costs.[1]

Matching practice addresses rolls the 12 clinics up under one owner. The list names the leaders it covers, such as the owner and the clinical director who leads the group's therapy work. Cost: included in the $11 above
- **Week 4. The group's leaders begin seeing our ads.** We split the list at random into two halves. This group falls in the half that receives account-based ads, purchased through Propensity, an account-based advertising platform. The other half, the holdout half, receives the same emails without ads, which lets us measure the incremental lift the ads deliver. The owner sees a five-card carousel ad on LinkedIn. Cost: about $45 over 90 days, comprising about $27 of media plus this group's share of Propensity's fee

Value: up to about $90,000 a year across 12 clinics

#### Learn more: the calculation

##### How the $6,900 is derived

At the base budget, month-three spend is about $33,300. The plan expects about 15 qualified calls a week at Day 90, but about 4 of those already arrive each week today, and about 2 come from the February conference, which has its own budget. The spend funds the remaining 9 or so a week, about 39 a month, so each costs about $860. Of qualified calls, 55% become sales opportunities, 45% of those start a pilot, and half of pilots sign. Overall, about 12.4% of calls convert to customers, and $860 divided by 0.124 is about $6,900 per new customer.

Across every call, including those this budget does not fund, the same month costs about $510 per call and $4,100 per customer. We use the higher figures because incremental spend only buys the calls it funds.

This group's own share is about $61 plus the box. The $6,900 also covers the groups that never buy, because about 8 qualified calls lead to each customer.

##### How the per-group costs are calculated

- List and data: the $10,800 data line spread over about 1,000 target groups is about $11 a group. It also funds scheduling tools and Salesforce work.
- Ads: the $22,500 account-based line spread over the roughly 500 groups in the half that sees ads is about $45 a group, comprising about $27 of media plus this group's share of Propensity's $9,000 fee.
- Email: the $5,400 email line spread over all 1,000 groups is about $5 a group.
- The direct mail box has no published price. Its price is a quote in week one, capped within the small-tests line.

## 2. The plan at a glance

**A $90,000 investment over 90 days secures three quarters of the target call volume.**

The goal is 20 qualified calls a week. Starting from Day 1 on December 1, 2026, the plan reaches about 15 a week by Day 90. Reaching 20 depends on the Day 90 decisions on budget and list size. Attribution traces every call to its source.

- **The goal: 20 qualified calls a week.** That volume fills the calendar of Tanner, the inside-sales representative who runs these calls, at about 20 a week.[1]

- **The budget: $90,000 for the first 90 days.** The budget covers marketing spend only. Salaries and the costs of the American Physical Therapy Association's Combined Sections Meeting (APTA CSM), the physical therapy conference in February, are funded separately. About 55% buys paid media and email, 37% funds video, web pages, the list and Salesforce setup, and 8% is held in reserve for the best-performing channels.

- **By Day 90: 15 qualified calls a week.** At that pace, Tanner's calendar is three quarters full by Day 90.

- **After Day 90: +5 qualified calls a week still needed to reach 20.** A second weekly live demo adds about 2 calls a week, and stronger email reply rates add about 1. The remaining calls, and new contacts once the list is exhausted around week 13, require more budget or more groups, which is decided at Day 90.

- **Cost per qualified call in month three: $860 for each call the budget funds, against a $930 limit.** $930 is the most a call can cost and still be covered by first-year payments, since about one call in eight becomes a $7,500 customer. Because $860 sits close to that limit, incremental spend goes only to channels that deliver calls below $930.

- **New customers: 24 a quarter (every three months), once the first pilots finish.** That volume represents about $180,000 in first-year value a quarter, about 1.8 times the roughly $100,000 a quarter required to sustain spend at the month-three pace, before any group adds clinics.

We track each lead through pilot to signed contract and report the cost at each stage.

It also creates a locked record of where each lead came from, pick lists for persona (job title), buying role, message variant (which message the lead received) and objection heard, a one-hour reply rule, pilot tracking and one weekly report.

## 4. The buying group

**Every sale requires four distinct approvals within the group.**

Within each physical therapy group, the people who make the decision together form its buying group. The owner signs, a clinical leader approves, a clinic director runs the pilot, and operations leaders decide on rollout.

- **Signs:** Owner or chief executive officer (CEO)
- **Approves clinically:** Chief clinical officer or clinical director
- **Runs the pilot:** Clinic director
- **Decides rollout and payment, and can block it:** Chief operating officer (COO) and practice administrator

Under 15 clinics, we start with the owner or CEO and the clinical director or a clinic director. From 15 clinics, we start with the chief clinical officer and the CEO.

#### Learn more: the buying group at each size

- **5 to 10 clinics: Contact first: The owner and one clinic director.** The owner often also serves as CEO and still treats patients. Each clinic has a director. A separate COO or chief clinical officer is rare.

- **11 to 14 clinics: Contact first: The owner or CEO and the clinical director.** The team includes an owner or CEO, a clinical director who leads clinical work, clinic directors and a practice administrator. Some groups also have a COO.

- **15 to 25 clinics: Contact first: The chief clinical officer and the CEO. The COO joins once a pilot is scoped.** The CEO, COO, chief clinical officer, clinic directors and practice administrator are each separate people.

Every group hears from at least one contact in the executive or operations cluster and one in the clinical cluster. From week 5, we test by group whether starting with the clinical contact or the executive books more qualified calls. ### Each cluster tests two messages, and a winner needs 30% more interested replies.

From week 5, people in each cluster are split at random between message A and message B, each written in the language of their own role. We call these the persona tests.

We read each test in week 8, once about 150 people have received each message. A message wins if its share of interested replies is at least 30% higher. If neither message leads, the test runs to six weeks and closes by February 15.

#### Learn more: the test design

##### The three tests

- **Executive cluster: Owner.** A: Patients who can see their progress at every visit have a reason to keep coming back. B: A return-to-sport service you can launch at one clinic, measure, then extend to the rest. CEO A: Track plan-of-care completion the same way across every clinic. - **Clinical cluster: Chief clinical officer.** A: Strength tests can miss compensation, when other muscles cover for an injured one. B: A documented return-to-sport protocol for the whole group. Clinical director A: See which muscles are doing the work, live, and coach the next rep. B: The same return-to-sport steps for every therapist in the group. Clinic director A: Show patients which muscles are doing the work during the test. B: Run the group's first documented return-to-sport protocol at your clinic.

##### How each test runs

- Within each cluster, contacts are randomly assigned to message A or message B through the Propensity ad audiences and the outbound email sequences.
- We read each test at about 150 contacts per side in week 8. If neither message leads by 30%, the test runs to six weeks, about 300 contacts a side, and closes by February 15, the end of week 11.
- We track two metrics: positive reply rate and meetings booked.
- A message wins only if its positive reply rate is at least 30% above the other's and meetings booked do not point the other way. If neither leads when the test closes, we record a tie, keep the simpler message and test a new challenger against it in month four.
- Volumes are small, so every win this quarter is provisional. Outbound reaches 300 contacts a week, which is 50 a week for each of the six test cells. At the planned 1.5% positive reply rate, 150 contacts yield about 2 replies a side, and six weeks yield about 4 or 5. A reliable win needs about 10 interested replies a side, or about 670 people per message, which this quarter cannot reach. We therefore apply each win provisionally, retest it in month four with fresh contacts, and treat a gap of one reply as a tie.

The calculation is 150 × 1.5% = 2.25 replies. Six weeks at 50 contacts a week gives 300 contacts a side, and 300 × 1.5% = 4.5 replies. Tests this small detect only large differences, which is why the bar is a 30% gap read on replies and meetings together, and why we recheck any small win.

##### Which contact comes first

In parallel, the demand generation lead tests contact order by group. Half of the groups in each size band start with a clinical contact, and the other half start with an executive. At Day 90, we compare qualified calls per 100 groups in each half. If one order leads by at least 30%, it becomes the default for next quarter's new groups. If neither does, both orders continue, and we compare pilots started per 100 groups at Day 180.

##### What sales records on every call

Four new Salesforce fields make the results measurable: persona, buying role, message variant and objection heard. Sales selects from a fixed list on every call. We review the fields weekly and update the answers and assets monthly.

## 5. Positioning and message

**Every message makes one point: NeuroStrip shows which muscles are doing the work.**

Strength tests measure how much force a leg or arm produces. NeuroStrip adds a live view of which muscles produce that force, on up to four muscles at once.

## 10. Lead handoff to sales

**Leads that pass three checks reach sales within one hour.**

This standard reserves Tanner's calendar for prospects who can buy and engages them while their interest is still high.

**1 hour** is the maximum response time for a sales-ready lead during business hours, set as a service-level agreement. The form displays a booking calendar as soon as it is submitted, so a lead who books there experiences no wait at all.

### Lead qualification criteria for sales-ready leads and qualified calls

1. The lead works at a target account: a physical therapy group with 5 to 25 clinics, or a professional or college sports program, in a state Control Bionics sells into.
2. The lead can approve a purchase or would use the product, and holds one of the seven roles in the buying group.
3. The lead requested a demo, a pilot or pricing, or replied to our email with interest.
4. The lead described, in their own words, a problem NeuroStrip helps solve: return-to-sport decisions, patients who stall or drop out, or a new service to offer.
5. The lead's group could start a 30-day pilot within 60 days.

A lead that passes checks 1 to 3 is sales-ready and is booked on Tanner's calendar. The call counts as a qualified call once sales confirms checks 4 and 5 during the conversation. The discovery questions for each role, under Learn more in the buying group section, draw out the problem, and the rep asks whether a 30-day pilot could start within the next 60 days. Leads from outside the target accounts are routed to the weekly live demo and the follow-up email sequence.

### Response-time service levels

- **0 min**The lead books a time on the calendar displayed immediately after the form.
- **15 min**Sales calls or emails any lead who did not book, during business hours.
- **1 hr**No lead waits longer than one hour. Any lead older than that appears in red on the weekly report.
- **1 day**Sales accepts or rejects each lead and selects a reason from a defined list.
- **6 tries**Sales makes six attempts over ten business days. If the lead does not respond, the lead returns to the follow-up email sequence.

### Weekly pipeline reporting tracks five metrics.

The demand generation lead distributes this report every Monday and reviews it with the sales lead. Each metric is segmented by channel, by buying role and by half of the ad test.

- **1: Qualified calls held.** This metric is measured against the goal of 20. It shows how full Tanner's calendar is.

- **2: Leads answered within one hour.** This metric is reported as a share of all sales-ready leads. Slow responses waste media spend.

- **3: Calls that became active deals (sales opportunities).** The plan assumes a conversion rate of 55 opportunities for every 100 qualified calls. If fewer than 45 convert for two consecutive weeks, sales and marketing tighten checks 4 and 5 that week.

- **4: Pilots started and pilots signed.** The plan assumes 45 of every 100 opportunities start a pilot and half of pilots sign. From 100 qualified calls, that yields about 12 new customers, worth about $90,000 in first-year revenue.

- **5: Cost per qualified call.** This metric is tracked by channel. About 12 of every 100 qualified calls become a $7,500 customer, so a call can cost up to about $930 and still be covered by first-year payments. A channel above $930 for two consecutive weeks forfeits its incremental budget to the reserve.

### A four-week Salesforce build tracks every lead from first click to signed contract.

Salesforce is the system sales uses to track every contact, call and deal. This build is the minimum configuration that shows which roles, messages and channels generate revenue. The demand generation lead owns the build and works with Control Bionics' Salesforce admin or a consultant, with that choice made by December 4.

- **Week 2.** Define the stages a lead moves through, each with a date.Record each lead's source from the link they clicked, in a field nobody can overwrite.Add "How did you hear about us?" to every form.Route new leads directly to sales with the one-hour response clock.

#### Learn more: the lead handoff

##### What sales receives with every lead

- The record shows practice size, role, persona and the lead's half of the test.
- The record names the ad, email or search term that produced the request.
- The record lists the person's last three actions, with dates.
- The record shows who else at the group has engaged.
- The record quotes the problem in the person's own words, from the form.

##### What marketing receives in return

- Sales accepts or rejects the lead within one business day and gives a reason.
- Sales records whether the call was held, missed or rescheduled.
- Sales selects the objection heard from the list.
- Sales records the pilot's start, end and result against the clinic's own measures.

##### How objection data improves the playbook

The demand generation lead and the sales lead review the objection field by role every Monday. Any objection heard three or more times without an effective answer receives a draft response that week. On the first Monday of each month, the team updates the answers and proof assets in the playbook, and any change that affects a claim goes to regulatory first.

##### When to add a second inside-sales seat

The sales lead requests a second inside-sales seat when either condition holds for two consecutive weeks: the next open call slot is more than five business days out, or fewer than 80% of leads receive a response within one hour. Leadership decides within one week of the request and reviews the decision again at Day 90.
