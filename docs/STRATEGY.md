# Filling the pipeline

**A 90-day plan to generate qualified demand for NeuroStrip**
Prepared by Kellen Levy for Control Bionics leadership. October 2026. Version 1.

Evidence labels used throughout:
**[V]** verified in a public source · **[S]** shared in our Sept 23 conversation · **[A]** planning assumption · **[H]** hypothesis the plan is built to test.
Full source list is at the end and in the site's evidence ledger.

---

## 1. Executive thesis

NeuroStrip does not have an awareness problem. It has a category problem. The people who should buy it already believe they measure readiness: force plates, dynamometers, hop tests, the clinician's eye. Nobody is searching for the thing NeuroStrip does, because nobody knows the gap exists. **[H]**

So the first 90 days do three things, in this order:

1. **Wire the revenue system first.** Every lead carries its audience, message, channel and campaign into Salesforce, through to opportunity and revenue. Month one is plumbing, not polish.
2. **Aim at one beachhead.** Sports and orthopedic outpatient physical therapy groups with 5 to 25 locations. That universe is roughly 1,000 accounts **[A]**, which makes this an account-based problem, not a lead-volume problem.
3. **Sell one idea through one conversion path.** The idea: *force shows what the body produced; NeuroStrip shows which muscle produced it.* The path: discovery call, 30-day pilot at one clinic, rollout across the group.

Elite sport supplies the proof. Physical therapy supplies the volume.

**The number that governs everything:** a repeatable 20 qualified discovery calls a week on the inside-sales calendar **[S]**. My base-case math reaches about 15 a week by Day 90 and 20 by roughly Day 120, with a stretch case that gets there by Day 90 **[A]**. I would rather show that math than promise the number.

**The philosophy:** run marketing the way NeuroStrip reads a muscle. Lead volume is the force plate: it can look healthy while the thing underneath is compensating. Qualified pipeline is the signal. We instrument the signal.

## 3. Audience recommendation (answers Question 1)

**Physical therapy first. Sport proves it. Not both equally.**

| Allocation | Lane | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **70%** | Regional PT groups, 5 to 25 locations, sports and ortho mix | The volume engine. Demand gen owns it end to end. |
| **20%** | Elite, pro and college sport | The proof lane. Business development hunts; demand gen supplies air cover and content. |
| **10%** | Everything else that arrives inbound (independent clinics, research, neuro rehab, performance facilities) | Capture and route. No outbound, no dedicated spend. |

**Why PT first**

1. **Capacity math.** 20 calls a week is about 1,000 conversations a year. Top-flight US pro sport is fewer than 200 teams and NCAA Division I is about 350 programs **[A]**. The 5 to 25 location PT band is about 10,000 clinics **[S]**, roughly 1,000 groups **[A]**, inside a market of about 39,000 outpatient clinics where 82% sit with independents and groups under 25 clinics **[V: s22]**. The head count says the same: about 284,000 physical therapists in the US against about 35,000 athletic trainers **[V: s24, s25]**.
2. **Land and expand.** One clinic becomes 25. Annual software plus equipment means the second through twenty-fifth site are the cheapest revenue Control Bionics will ever win.
3. **A reachable decision maker.** A clinician still sits on the leadership team and can say yes **[S]**.
4. **Division of labor.** A business development lead is the right tool for a few hundred elite logos. A demand engine is the right tool for 1,000 regional groups.
5. **Precedent.** VALD took the same route: elite sport first for profile, then physiotherapy clinics for scale, where health reached a quarter of sales **[V: s38]**.

**Why not sport first.** Sport scores higher on urgency and on how fast the demo lands. It scores lowest on scale and on paid reachability. It cannot feed 20 calls a week, and its buying calendar is seasonal.

**Why not both equally.** Two audiences means two message systems, two proof libraries and two sets of landing pages, built by one person in 90 days. Splitting evenly guarantees both are thin.

**ICP tiers**

- **Tier A (1:1 discovery call):** PT group with 5 to 25 locations, sports or ortho caseload, decision maker or clinical lead. Pro and college sports medicine or performance leads.
- **Tier B (weekly live group demo first):** PT groups with 2 to 4 or 26 to 50 locations, performance facility owners, neuro rehab program leads.
- **Tier C (nurture, self-serve content):** solo clinics, practitioners without authority (still valuable as champions), unfunded researchers.
- **Tier D (no sales time):** students, patients, vendors, unserviceable regions.

**Account signals that move a Tier A account to the front of the line [H]:** a return-to-sport testing page on their website, force plates or dynamometers already in use, blood flow restriction or other tech-forward services, a sports residency, athletic trainer outreach contracts, recent new locations.

**Where I am deliberately pushing on the brief.** The marketing entry point is not only the leadership team. Pilots live or die on a clinical champion, so every campaign runs two threads into the same account: the clinician who wants to see the trace, and the executive who needs the economics.

## 4. Positioning and demand thesis

**What they believe the problem is.** Patients plateau or drop off. Athletes re-injure. Reimbursement is squeezed and it is hard to stand out.

**What they do not realize yet.** The tests they trust measure output. Compensation hides inside output. A limb can pass on force while the injured muscle is still under-recruiting.

**What they use instead.** Force plates, handheld dynamometers, hop tests, manual muscle testing, a one or two channel biofeedback box in a drawer (mTrigger from $499 **[V: s32]**, Kinvent K-Myo at $2,790 for two sensors **[V: s33]**), or a research EMG rig that needs a lab and a specialist (EUR 10,000 to 25,000+ **[V: s34, vendor guide]**).

**Why NeuroStrip is different.** Lab-grade signal with bandage-level effort: four muscles at once, no wires, live on an iPad, with a record you can document.

**Positioning statement.** For clinicians and performance staff who decide when someone is ready, NeuroStrip is the wearable activation sensor that shows which muscles are doing the work, live, in the clinic or on the field. Force plates and dynamometers measure output. Lab EMG needs a lab. NeuroStrip goes on like a bandage and puts activation next to the measures you already trust.

**The line.** *Force shows what the body produced. NeuroStrip shows which muscle produced it.*

**The supporting evidence we can cite without overclaiming**
- In elite men's football, hamstring injuries rose from 12% to 24% of all injuries over 21 seasons; 18% were recurrences, and more than two thirds of those happened within two months of return to play. **[V: s16]**
- Nearly 1 in 4 athletes under 25 who return to high-risk sport after ACL reconstruction sustain a second ACL injury (23%). **[V: s17]**
- "Traditional functional assessments alone may fail to detect underlying AMI-related impairments." **[V: s18]**
- A systematic review of eight randomized trials found EMG biofeedback more effective than comparison rehab for quadriceps strength after knee surgery in most studies, while noting high-quality evidence is limited. **[V: s19]**
- An industry-cited figure: about 20% of PT patients drop out within three visits and 70% do not complete their course of care. **[V: s20, industry source, 2017]**

**Claims guardrail.** NeuroStrip is a non-diagnostic measurement and biofeedback tool **[V: s8]**. Copy never says diagnose, prevent, predict or guarantee. Every outcome number is tied to a named, consented source and approved against a claims matrix before it ships.

## 6. Channel strategy and budget (answers Question 2)

**Three channels, three jobs.**

**LinkedIn (named accounts): create demand.** The only paid platform that can target the roughly 1,000-account universe by company list and title. Small audience, so a modest budget saturates it. Its direct cost per call will look poor on its own **[A]**; its real job is making outbound and search convert better, and that is tested with a holdout.

**Google Search: capture demand.** Category terms (sEMG biofeedback, wireless EMG sensor), competitor terms and problem terms (return to sport testing, quad activation after ACL). Low volume, high intent, live in week two, and it teaches us the market's own words.

**Email (coordinated outbound plus nurture): convert.** The outbound program already running **[S]** gets re-briefed around named-account tiers, signal-based triggers and Salesforce logging. Every inbound lead gets a persona and stage-specific nurture.

Supporting, not a test channel: a small retargeting layer on Meta and YouTube. Events (APTA CSM, Feb 4 to 6, 2027, Philadelphia **[V: s26]**) are treated as conversion moments with their own budget.

**Channel budget split:** LinkedIn 45% · Search 20% · Email infrastructure and data 10% · Retargeting 10% · Asymmetric bets 15%.

**Whole 90-day program budget:**

| Line | Share |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 25% |
| Google Search | 11% |
| Email and outbound orchestration | 6% |
| Retargeting | 5% |
| Asymmetric bets (educator and newsletter sponsorships, partner co-marketing, booth activation) | 8% |
| Proof assets and creative production | 25% |
| Data, tooling and measurement | 12% |
| Reserve released at the Day 30 and Day 60 gates | 8% |

Only about half of the program budget is media in the first 90 days. Media share of monthly spend climbs from about one third in month one, to two thirds in month two, to more than four fifths in month three. Scale is earned, not assumed.

**Dollar scenarios [A]:** Lean $45K, Base $90K, Aggressive $150K for the 90 days, excluding salaries, event costs and the existing outbound retainer. Budget was not disclosed; the percentages are the recommendation.

**Guardrail metric.** Allowable cost per held, qualified call equals first-year contract value times the call-to-customer rate. At a $7,500 contract and a 12% call-to-customer rate that is about $930; at $3,500 it is about $430 **[A]**. Every channel is judged against that, not against cost per lead.

**Kill and scale rules** are pre-committed per channel (see the site). Example: if by Day 60 LinkedIn's direct cost per call is above twice the allowable figure and the holdout shows less than a 20% lift in outbound reply rate, prospecting spend drops to retargeting only and the money moves to search, live demos and events.

## 7. Creative testing system

**Territories, ranked**

1. **The trace** (demonstration). The product's "aha" is visual and takes five seconds.
2. **Output is not activation** (reframe). "Passed the strength test. Still compensating."
3. **A clinic like yours** (peer proof). "The NeuroStrip system is the most impactful advancement I've seen in 30 years." (Steve, PT, 30+ years, Houston Rehabilitation Institute) Paired with a group mid-rollout. **[V: s3]**
4. **EMG left the basement** (old way versus new way).
5. **Visible progress** (economics for executives). "Patients don't quit therapy. They quit when they can't see progress."
6. **Elite sport halo.** A proof line, never the lead message for PT.

**Deliberately not in the first 90 days:** independence and human-story work (it belongs to the assistive communication brand), headline performance-gain numbers (they need tighter substantiation and suit a narrower audience), patient testimonials (privacy and consumer-claims exposure), fear-led injury-prevention copy (outside what a non-diagnostic tool should claim), and neurotech futurism (an investor story, not a buyer story).

**The loop.** Hypothesis, creative, audience, channel, result, learning, new hypothesis. One variable at a time, with a spend cap and a decision rule written before launch.

**The first proof sprint (weeks 2 to 4).** One 90-second trace explainer, two customer stories shot on site, one field guide, one clinic economics model, one pilot blueprint. These six assets feed every channel.

## 8. Lead qualification (answers Question 4)

**A qualified lead is a right-fit account with a named problem and a path to a pilot.**

| Stage | Definition |
|---|---|
| Inquiry | Any identified person who engages in a tracked way. |
| Lead | A valid person in a business context, with segment and tier assigned. |
| Engaged lead | Meaningful activity in the last 30 days. |
| MQL | Fit A or B, plus a hand-raise or sustained engagement. Routed with an SLA. |
| SAL | Accepted by inside sales within one business day, or rejected with a reason. |
| SQL | Discovery call held and the PILOT checklist met. Becomes an opportunity. |
| Opportunity | Discovery, validation demo, pilot proposed, pilot active, commercial proposal, closed. |
| Customer | Paid equipment and software. Then tracked by sites live versus total sites. |

**Two questions, not twenty.** Fit (A to D) comes from segment, size, role and geography. Intent (1 to 3) comes from behavior. Fit A or B with a hand-raise books straight onto the calendar.

**The PILOT checklist for sales (4 of 5, first two mandatory):**
**P**roblem named · **I**CP fit · **L**eader identified · **O**perator named (the clinician who will run it) · **T**iming inside 60 days.

**Measured through revenue:** held qualified calls per week, sales-accepted rate, SQL rate, pilot starts, pilot to paid, cycle time, cost per each, pipeline and revenue by audience, message, channel and campaign. Cost per lead is diagnostic only. A falling CPL is a warning until the sales-accepted rate confirms it.

## 9. Salesforce in month one (answers Question 5)

1. **Week 1, audit and separate.** What exists in Sales Cloud and Account Engagement. NeuroStrip gets its own record types, page layouts and sales process, and assistive communication patient records are excluded from every marketing audience.
2. **Lifecycle stage with a timestamp for every stage.** Without timestamps there is no velocity and no conversion reporting.
3. **Source capture that survives.** Locked lead-source picklist, first-touch and last-touch UTM fields, click IDs, and a "how did you hear about us" field on every hand-raise form.
4. **Campaign hierarchy** that mirrors the reporting question: period, audience, message territory, channel, asset. Standard member statuses.
5. **Account-centric structure.** Parent and child accounts for multi-location groups, number of locations, tier, lead-to-account matching, buying-group contact roles.
6. **Routing and SLA.** Assignment rules, instant scheduling on hand-raise forms, a speed-to-lead timer, required disqualification reasons.
7. **NeuroStrip opportunity stages with pilot tracking.** Exit criteria, required primary campaign source, pilot start and end dates, success criteria, controlled loss reasons.
8. **Fit grade and engagement score** using Account Engagement's native grading and scoring. Version one, deliberately simple.
9. **Five dashboards:** the calendar (calls booked and held versus 20), funnel and velocity, speed to lead, source to pipeline to revenue, pilot board.
10. **Backfill the trial base.** Every trial from the last 12 months goes in with segment, source, outcome and reason. That gives real conversion and cycle-time baselines on day 30 instead of day 180.
11. **Close the loop to the ad platforms.** Qualified-stage events flow back to LinkedIn and Google so they optimize toward sales-accepted calls, not form fills.

The question the system must answer: *which audiences, messages, channels and campaigns produce revenue?*

## 11. 90-day roadmap

**Days 1 to 30, Instrument.** Listen to calls and interview 8 to 10 trial customers. Audit Salesforce, the site and tracking. Lock tiers, definitions and the SLA with sales. Fix the conversion path. Search goes live in week two. Proof sprint. Trial backfill. Soft launch on LinkedIn.
*Gate:* a test lead travels from ad to opportunity with its source intact; baselines exist; first campaigns have 10 days of data.

**Days 31 to 60, Learn.** Two message territories against two personas. Outbound with a LinkedIn holdout. Weekly live demo. Offer test. CSM meeting booking opens.
*Gate:* kill the bottom half; pick the winning territory and offer; release the reserve.

**Days 61 to 90, Scale.** Winners up 25% a week inside frequency caps. APTA CSM with a booth activation built around a 60-second asymmetry test **[H]**. Qualified-stage feedback to the ad platforms. First pilot conversions read. Playbook written.
*Gate:* unit economics by source; decision on Q2 budget, a second inside-sales seat and the second segment.

If Day 1 is December 1, the holiday weeks become the build window, the full launch lands the first week of January, and CSM falls on Days 66 to 68.

## 12. Experimentation framework

Twelve experiments are queued and scored (impact, confidence, ease). The first five:

1. Hidden deficit versus visible progress as the lead message for PT executives.
2. Executive-first versus clinician-first entry into the same accounts.
3. Pilot scoping call versus live group demo versus economics model as the primary offer.
4. LinkedIn air cover holdout: does it lift outbound reply rate?
5. Gated versus ungated field guide.

**Decision rules.** Kill at the spend cap if the leading metric is under half of control or the ICP rate is under 20%. Continue if within 20% of control. Scale at 30% better than control with no drop in sales-accepted rate.

**Honest statistics.** At these volumes nothing reaches significance on leads. We test big differences, read the deepest metric that has enough volume for the phase, and write the rule before the launch.

## 13. Risks and assumptions

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| Claims drift beyond a non-diagnostic tool | Claims matrix approved with regulatory before launch |
| Thin public proof for PT | Month-one proof sprint; trial base as the story pipeline |
| One inside-sales calendar, finite pilot kits | Tiered routing, weekly group demo, capacity triggers for hire two |
| Small universe, fast fatigue | Frequency caps, monthly creative refresh, deliberate tier expansion |
| Attribution blind spots | Three lenses: click data, self-reported source, account-level holdouts |
| "EMG" read as a needle test or an old biofeedback box | Education before product in the sequence |
| Price gap to low-cost biofeedback | Sell four muscles, no wires, records and program economics; never lead on price |
| Outbound domain health | Separate domains, warm-up, bounce and complaint thresholds |
| Patient data sitting near marketing data | Record-type separation and audience exclusions |
| December start | Build in December, launch in January |

Key assumptions to validate in week one: budget, Salesforce edition and Account Engagement licence, inside-sales capacity, pilot kit pool, pilot length, account universe size, and every conversion rate in the model.

## 16. What makes this different from a typical candidate deck

1. It works backward from the inside-sales calendar and its constraints, not forward from a list of channels.
2. It treats a 1,000-account market as account-based, and says so.
3. Every claim is labeled verified, shared, assumed or hypothesized, with sources.
4. Leadership can change the weights and the math and watch the recommendation hold or break.
5. It uses what Control Bionics already has: 12 months of trials as baseline data, what looks like an Account Engagement instance already in place, an outbound program, public reference customers, and CSM inside the window.
6. Kill criteria are written before the money is spent.
7. The plan is the work sample.
