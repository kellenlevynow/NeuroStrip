## 1. Follow one group

**Winning a 12-clinic group's first clinic takes about 13 weeks and, on average, about $6,900 of marketing.**

Here is how an example group, a made-up physical therapy (PT) company that owns 12 clinics, would move through the plan, and what each step costs at the plan's planning numbers. The rest of the page explains the system behind it.

**$6,900** of marketing, on average, to win one new customer from the calls the recommended $90,000 budget pays for. We plan on one clinic paying Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, about $625 a month, so the $6,900 is covered by about 11 months of that clinic's payments, before product, sales and pilot kit costs.[1]

- **Week 2. The group is on our list.** We buy a list from a database of healthcare providers, which finds clinics by their National Provider Identifier (NPI). We group the 12 clinics under one owner by matching their practice addresses. The list names the leaders it holds, such as the owner and the rehabilitation (rehab) director who leads the group's therapy work. Which job titles each list covers is checked in week one. Cost: about $11, this group's share of the $10,800 data budget. List and Bombora prices are quotes in week one.
- **Week 3. People there start researching.** Bombora, an intent data company, shows that staff at the group are reading more than usual about rehab equipment and billing software. Bombora reports by company and does not name people. That marks the group as researching now, so it moves to the front of the line for ads and email. Cost: already counted in the $11 above
- **Week 4. Its leaders start to see our ads.** The list is split at random into two halves. This group lands in the half that sees account-based ads, bought through Propensity, an account-based ad platform. The other half, the holdout half, gets the same emails without ads, so we can measure what the ads add. The owner sees a five-card carousel ad on LinkedIn. The clinic directors see the 15-second video, and banner ads on websites in Propensity's network. Cost: about $45 over 90 days, about $27 of ads plus this group's share of Propensity's fee
- **Week 5. The rehab director and the owner each get an email.** The rehab director receives the first of four short, plain emails, and the owner receives the first owner email. Had the owner not replied by week 8, a box would follow with a sample strip and a one-page estimate built on labeled planning figures the group can replace with its own. Cost: about $5 of email. The box price is a quote in week one.
- **Week 6. The rehab director books a call.** The rehab director replies and books a time on the calendar of Tanner, the inside-sales rep who takes these calls. The lead passes our five checks, so the call counts as one qualified call, the number this whole plan is built to grow. Cost: about $860 of marketing per qualified call the budget pays for in month three, just under the $930 a call can cost and still be covered by first-year payments
- **Week 7. They agree to a 30-day pilot.** On the call, the rehab director, the owner and one clinic director agree to a pilot, a 30-day trial at one clinic. Before it starts, they write their own success measures, such as minutes added to a visit and how often therapists use it. Cost: sales time, outside this budget
- **Weeks 8 to 12. One clinic runs the pilot.** The clinic director uses NeuroStrip with real patients for 30 days. The rehab director checks the results against the measures they wrote. Cost: one kit on loan from the set Control Bionics keeps for pilots, plus setup time, both outside this budget. Pilot terms: confirm in week one.
- **Week 13. The owner signs for the first clinic.** The pilot met its measures, so the owner signs. That clinic is planned to pay Control Bionics about $7,500 in its first year, more than the $6,900 it took, on average, to win.[3] Value: about $7,500 in the first year
- **Months 4 to 12. The group rolls it out to more clinics.** The group's chief operating officer (COO) plans the next clinics on the same contract. If each extra clinic were priced like the first, all 12 would bring in about $90,000 a year with no new marketing spend. The price per extra clinic is confirmed in week one. Value: up to about $90,000 a year across 12 clinics

#### Learn more: the math

##### Where the $6,900 comes from

At the base budget, month three spends about $33,300. The plan expects about 15 qualified calls a week at Day 90, but about 4 of those already come in today and about 2 come from the February conference, which has its own budget. The spend pays for about 9 a week, or about 39 a month, so each costs about $860. Of qualified calls, 55% become real sales opportunities, 45% of those start a pilot, and half of pilots sign. So about 12.4% of calls become customers, and $860 divided by 0.124 is about $6,900 per new customer.

Counting every call, including the ones this budget does not pay for, the same month costs about $510 a call and $4,100 a customer. We use the higher figures, because new money only buys the calls it pays for.

This group's own share is about $61 plus the box. The $6,900 also pays for the groups that never buy: about 8 qualified calls lead to each customer.

##### How the per-group costs are worked out

- List and data: the $10,800 data line spread over about 1,000 target groups is about $11 a group. It also pays for scheduling tools and Salesforce work.
- Ads: the $22,500 account-based line spread over the roughly 500 groups in the half that sees ads is about $45 a group, about $27 of ads plus this group's share of Propensity's $9,000 fee.
- Email: the $5,400 email line spread over all 1,000 groups is about $5 a group.
- The direct mail box has no published price. It is quoted in week one and capped inside the small-tests line.

## 4. The buying group

**Every sale needs four kinds of yes inside the group.**

Inside each physical therapy group, the people who decide together are its buying group. The owner signs, a clinical leader approves, a clinic director runs the pilot, and the people who run operations decide if it can roll out.

- **Signs:** Owner or chief executive officer (CEO)
- **Approves clinically:** Chief clinical officer or rehab director
- **Runs the pilot:** Clinic director
- **Decides rollout and payment, and can block it:** Chief operating officer (COO) and practice administrator

Who to contact first depends on size. Every group hears from at least one contact in the executive or operations cluster and one in the clinical cluster. Under 15 clinics, start with the owner or CEO and the rehab director or a clinic director. From 15 clinics, start with the chief clinical officer and the CEO.

#### Learn more: who is there at each size

- **5 to 10 clinics: Contact first: The owner and one clinic director.** The owner is often also the CEO and still treats patients. Each clinic has a director. A separate COO or chief clinical officer is rare.

- **11 to 14 clinics: Contact first: The owner or CEO and the rehab director.** An owner or CEO, a rehab director who leads clinical work, clinic directors and a practice administrator. A COO sometimes appears.

- **15 to 25 clinics: Contact first: The chief clinical officer and the CEO. The COO joins once a pilot is scoped.** A CEO, a COO, a chief clinical officer, clinic directors and a practice administrator, each a separate person.

### We test two messages on each cluster and keep one only if it earns at least 30% more interested replies.

From week 5, people in each cluster are split at random between message A and message B, each in their own role's wording. We call these the persona tests.

Each test is read in week 8, once about 150 people have had each message. A message wins if its share of interested replies is at least 30% higher. If neither is ahead, the test runs to six weeks and closes by February 15.

#### Learn more: the test design

##### The three tests

- **Executive cluster: Owner.** A: Patients who can see their progress at each visit have a reason to keep coming back. B: A return-to-sport service you can start at one clinic, measure, then add to the rest. CEO A: Track plan-of-care completion the same way in every clinic. B: One program you can roll out to every clinic and measure.

- **Operations cluster: COO.** A: Each sensor goes on like a bandage, and the pilot times setup at one clinic. B: One page with the annual price and a rollout plan for each new clinic. Practice administrator A: Setup and office time, both timed in the pilot. B: One page that lists what the annual price includes.

- **Clinical cluster: Chief clinical officer.** A: Strength tests can miss muscles covering for an injured one. B: A documented return-to-sport protocol for the whole group. Rehab director A: See which muscles do the work, live, and coach the next rep. B: The same return-to-sport steps for every therapist in the group. Clinic director A: Show patients which muscles are doing the work during the test. B: Run the group's first documented return-to-sport protocol at your clinic.

##### How each test runs

- Inside each cluster, contacts are split at random between message A and message B, through the Propensity ad audiences and the outbound email sequences.
- Each test is read at about 150 contacts per side, in week 8. If neither message is 30% ahead, it runs to six weeks, about 300 a side, and closes by February 15, the end of week 11.
- We read two numbers: positive reply rate and meetings booked.
- A message wins only if its positive reply rate is 30% or more above the other, and meetings booked do not point the other way. If neither is ahead when the test closes, it is a tie: we keep the simpler message and test a new one against it in month four.
- Volumes are small, so every win this quarter is provisional. Outbound reaches 300 contacts a week, which is 50 a week for each of the six test cells. At the planned 1.5% positive reply rate, 150 contacts give about 2 replies a side and six weeks give about 4 or 5. A firm win needs about 10 interested replies a side, about 670 people a message, which this quarter cannot reach. So every win is used for now and tested again in month four with fresh contacts, and a gap of one reply counts as a tie.

The math: 150 × 1.5% = 2.25 replies. Six weeks at 50 contacts a week is 300 contacts a side, and 300 × 1.5% = 4.5 replies. Tests this small only find large differences, which is why the bar is a 30% gap read on replies and meetings together, and why a small win is checked again.

##### Who to contact first

At the same time, the demand generation lead tests the order of contact by group. Half of the groups in each size band start with a clinical contact, and the other half start with an executive. At Day 90 we compare qualified calls per 100 groups in each half. If one order is at least 30% ahead, it becomes the default for next quarter's new groups. If not, both orders stay, and we read pilots started per 100 groups at Day 180.

##### What sales records

Four new Salesforce fields make this readable: persona, buying role, message variant and objection heard. Sales picks from a fixed list on every call. We review them every week and update the answers and assets every month.

## 5. What we say

Strength tests show how much force a leg or arm makes. NeuroStrip adds a live view of which muscles make it, on up to four muscles at once.

## 10. Handing leads to sales

**A lead gets a sales call after three checks, and hears back within an hour.**

That keeps Tanner's calendar for people who can buy, and gets to them while they are still interested.

**1 hour** the longest a sales-ready lead waits for a reply in business hours. The form shows a calendar as soon as it is sent, so a lead who books there waits no time at all.

### What makes a lead sales-ready, and a call qualified

1. Works at a target group: a physical therapy group with 5 to 25 clinics, or a pro or college sports program, in a state we sell into.
2. Can approve a purchase or would use the product: one of the seven roles in the buying group.
3. Asked for a demo, a pilot or pricing, or replied to our email with interest.
4. Named a problem we help with, in their own words: return-to-sport decisions, patients who stall or drop out, or a new service to offer.
5. Could start a 30-day pilot within 60 days.

A lead that passes checks 1 to 3 is sales-ready and goes on Tanner's calendar. The call counts as a qualified call once sales confirms checks 4 and 5 on it: the discovery questions for that role, under Learn more in the buying group section, draw out the problem, and the rep asks whether a 30-day pilot could start in the next 60 days. Leads from outside the target groups go to the weekly live demo and the follow-up emails.

### How fast we reply

- **0 min**The lead picks a time on a calendar shown right after the form.
- **15 min**A call or email if they did not book, in business hours.
- **1 hr**No lead waits longer. Anything older shows in red on the weekly report.
- **1 day**Sales accepts or rejects each lead, with a reason picked from a list.
- **6 tries**Sales tries six times over ten business days. With no answer, the lead goes back to the follow-up emails.

### We report five numbers every week.

The demand generation lead sends this report every Monday and reviews it with the sales lead. Each number is split by channel, by role and by half of the ad test.

- **1: Qualified calls held.** Against the goal of 20. It shows how full Tanner's calendar is.

- **2: Leads answered within an hour.** As a share of all sales-ready leads. Slow replies waste the spend.

- **3: Calls that became real deals (sales opportunities).** Planned at 55 of every 100 qualified calls. If fewer than 45 become opportunities for two weeks in a row, sales and marketing tighten checks 4 and 5 that week.

- **4: Pilots started and pilots signed.** 45 of every 100 opportunities start a pilot, and half of pilots sign. From 100 qualified calls, that is about 12 new customers, worth about $90,000 in their first year.

- **5: Cost per qualified call.** By channel. About 12 of every 100 qualified calls become a $7,500 customer, so a call can cost up to about $930 and still be covered by first-year payments. A channel above $930 for two weeks in a row loses its extra money to the reserve.
